# Related Work (draft)

Citation keys match `references.bib`. Every empirical claim attributed below was checked against the source's abstract or page. The final paragraph states our positioning and makes no claim about results.

**Decomposition and composition attacks.** A growing line of work shows that refusals tested one request at a time can be bypassed by splitting a harmful goal into subtasks that look benign. Jones et al. show that an adversary can assign subtasks to different models so that the combination misuses even when each model passes safety checks alone [jones2024combinations]. Yueh-Han et al. report an 87% average attack success rate on GPT-4o for decomposition attacks and propose a sequential monitor that tracks cumulative intent [yuehan2025monitoring]. Srivastav and Zhang's REALM 2025 paper is the closest prior work to our pipeline condition: a multi-agent, multi-turn framework with a Question Decomposer, a Sub-Question Answerer and an Answer Combiner splits a harmful query into seemingly benign sub-questions, and the authors report attack success rates often exceeding 90% on the models tested (GPT-3.5-Turbo, Gemma-2-9B, Mistral-7B among them) [srivastav2025safe]. Their setting is an attack against safety mechanisms: the decomposition is part of the attacker's method, whereas we use a synthetic operator restriction and study how local context affects each restriction-bound agent's behaviour. Ahad et al. describe Semantic Intent Fragmentation, in which a single legitimately phrased request leads an LLM orchestrator to split the task into subtasks that each pass subtask-level checks while the composed plan violates policy (10 of 14 scenarios with a GPT-20B orchestrator); there the composition emerges from the orchestrator rather than from an injected payload, and the authors propose plan-level information-flow checks [ahad2026semantic]. For agents, DeCompBench finds that state-of-the-art agents often refuse whole harmful tasks but refuse the decomposed versions much less [kothamasu2026decompbench]; related benchmarks cover code [wahed2025mocha] and covert misuse more generally [brown2025covert]. Russinovich et al. describe "capability laundering", in which a weaker model splits a task, consults an aligned model on each piece and assembles the answers itself [russinovich2026laundering]. In most of these works the split is designed by or for an adversary who knows the goal (a user or attacker-controlled decomposer), and the models that answer the pieces are treated as tools. Semantic Intent Fragmentation is a partial exception, since the harmful split is produced by the orchestrator itself, but it still starts from an attacker's request. Single-model work shows the same effect when an attacker fragments a prompt [li2024drattack] or escalates over turns by referencing the model's own replies [russinovich2024crescendo]. Agent-harm benchmarks such as AgentHarm measure refusal of explicitly malicious single-agent tasks [andriushchenko2024agentharm]; they do not vary what each agent knows about the goal.

**Context-dependent disclosure.** ConfAIde tests whether models reason about what to share, with whom and for what purpose; GPT-4 and ChatGPT revealed private information in contexts where humans would not 39% and 57% of the time, even with privacy-inducing prompts [mireshghallah2024confaide]. Our framing manipulation is a minimal version of this question for an operator restriction.

**Multi-agent safety and collusion.** A second line studies how safety properties change when agents interact. Hammond et al. give a taxonomy of three failure modes and seven risk factors, among them information asymmetries and emergent agency [hammond2025multiagent]. Shen et al. find that organizations of aligned models produce solutions with higher utility but greater misalignment than a single aligned model [shen2026aiorgs]. On the monitoring side, production blocking monitors fail to block over 55% of red-team attacks, and spreading a side task across agents or compactions so that no single context contains a blockable action is among the most effective strategies [remedios2026automode]; behaviour that is benign in single interactions can be harmful in aggregate [sumers2025summarization]. AI control evaluates protocols against deliberately subversive models [greenblatt2024control]; our agents are not adversarial. Prompt infection and contagious jailbreaks spread an attacker's payload between agents [lee2024prompt, men2024troublemaker], and NetSafe reports that network topology affects how readily attacks spread [yu2024netsafe]. Collusion work concerns agents that deliberately coordinate in hidden ways, for example through steganography [motwani2024secret], and proposes detectors based on model internals [rose2026narc]. Positive-coordination work shows that groups of LLM agents can form functional collectives with complementary roles [riedl2025emergent, guo2024embodied] and can be studied in shared-resource simulations [piatti2024cooperate]. Protocol-level analyses of A2A and MCP focus on identity, delegation and trust between agents [habler2025a2a, prakash2026aip, hu2025trust, hu2026dissociative, ferrag2025threats]. Much of this work, though not all of it, assumes an adversarial agent or protocol participant. The Apart sprint project by Chen (Team DLab) is a different case: it reports that agents refused direct requests for restricted records in nearly all cases yet broke a rule in 22-72% of games once a sanctioned tool degraded, often jointly [chen2026theysay]. It is early sprint work, not peer reviewed, and concerns goal pressure under a failing tool rather than lookup-and-concatenate decomposition.

**The OpenAI / Hugging Face incident.** METR's investigation reports that about 1,200 OpenAI agents, meant to be isolated, used an internal Artifactory cache as a message board, and that about 700 went on to participate in an attack on Hugging Face [metr2026incident, metr2026report]. In the sample the authors examined, about a quarter of attack-joining agents cited a direct assignment or division of labour, and expressed ethical concerns "only rarely materially limited agents' actions" [metr2026incident]. Independent follow-ups released partial public data on the swarm's output [forman2026swarmtraces] and on related agent activity on a public scanning service [cable2026urlquery]. Prior Apart sprint work also touches the area: an omission-attacks project argues that action-level monitoring can miss harms that appear only as missing obligations [kemeklis2026omission], and the AI Collusion Sprint page lists a project on agents that decline individually but combine restricted records [apart2026collusionsprint]. **Positioning.** Prior decomposition attacks typically evaluate an adversary who knows the forbidden goal and splits it across models, turns or agents; prior multi-agent safety work often studies attacker-initiated contagion or deliberate collusion, with exceptions such as orchestrator-generated fragmentation [ahad2026semantic] and the sprint project above. Our question is narrower and more modest: does a restriction-bound agent's refusal depend on whether its *local context* links its piece to the restricted whole, and does splitting the task across agents change system-level disclosure or assembly relative to a single agent given equal opportunity (K independent attempts)? We compare a pipeline, a fixed round-robin shared board, and a coordinator relaying sub-requests, with the same policy for every role. We do not claim that earlier work conflates agent-level and system-level safety, nor that a participating adversary is always required; we claim only that our controlled, string-scored proxy with opportunity-matched baselines measures a context-sensitivity question that those papers do not isolate. The incident above motivates the setting but reports no refusal-alone baseline [metr2026incident], and the sources used here do not either, so any composition effect we report must come from our own frozen v2 runs. Our restriction is a synthetic operator instruction, not trained-in refusal, so the closeness of our setting to the decomposition-attack literature is limited.
